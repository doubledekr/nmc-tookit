Getting started with the NMC Toolkit

The toolkit is a desktop app for Windows and Mac. Every calculation, chart, parser and PDF export runs on your own computer — no internet needed for the core work.

Download the newest version

Download for Windows.exe installer Download for Mac.dmg

These buttons always point at the latest release. Already installed? You don't need to download again — the app updates itself.

  1. 1

    Install the app

    Windows: run the downloaded NMC-Toolkit-Setup file. Windows may say it's an "unrecognized app" — click More info → Run anyway. Mac: open the .dmg and drag NMC Toolkit into Applications; the first time, right-click the app → Open. After that it updates itself: when a new version is out you'll see an Update bar — click it, or it installs the next time you quit. Your data lives with the app and survives updates.

  2. 2

    Fill in the welcome card

    Your name, NMLS #, direct line and email. These print on every proposal. The NMC main line (844-210-3644) is built in.

  3. 3

    Set your save file and the home office

    Settings → Your save file & the home office. Pick your save folder and paste the hub address IT gives you. A green dot means the hub is reachable. Do this once — after that, saving, rate updates, team numbers and templates are automatic.

  4. 4

    Add the Salesforce bookmark

    One-time setup that lets you pull a lead into the toolkit with a click — see Salesforce bookmark.

  5. 5

    Moving from the browser version?

    Use Settings → Import to bring in an old JSON export. Nothing is lost.

Off the VPN? Pipeline, client records, analysis and proposals keep working off your local copy. Only shared things (rate pushes, team stats, the template library) pause, and they catch up within a couple of minutes of reconnecting — including uploading anything you did offline.

Your day

The screens, in the order most bankers use them.

Pipeline

The bell bar shows who's hot right now and which follow-ups are due. Filter with Hot now, Follow-ups due or Watching. Close a follow-up with an outcome (done, no answer, reschedule) and pick the next step. When a rate push opens a streamline window, you get a notification with names.

Client workspace

Paste the Salesforce record, confirm the current loan and debts. Each client keeps an activity log and a proposal library of everything you've sent them.

Savings analysis & proposal

Run the numbers for the client you have open, compare options side by side, and send a branded proposal as an image, email or PDF. See Working a client.

Tools

The mid-call calculator deck — no client needed. Current vs new, rate ladder, break-even, skipped-payment cash, cash available from the home, and how long a card takes at its minimum.

Scripts & templates

Company texts, intros and emails published by leadership appear automatically, with the client's and your details filled in. Save your own; share the good ones and leadership can promote them company-wide.

My desk

Your info, save-file status, today's rates with the 10-yr Treasury direction and rate history, and your month: loans closed, volume, folder pace, pipeline counts.

Watch it start to finish

    1. 1

      Paste the Salesforce record

      On the open lead in Salesforce, click your Copy lead for NMC bookmark (or copy the profile by hand), then in the toolkit hit Paste from clipboard & parse. You get a current → new preview of every field; check what you want and Apply. A paste always creates a new client unless it matches one by phone or email and you choose to update. SSN, DOB, age and marital status are refused and never stored.

    2. 2

      Confirm the current loan

      Balance, rate and months remaining drive everything. If you only have the original amount, the toolkit back-solves months remaining (30-year term by default). Add home value for LTV.

    3. 3

      Paste lender pricing

      Paste a lender's rate stack; the parser reads rate, price or points, and lock period. Save the options you'd offer, then click one to use its rate in the analysis.

    4. 4

      Add debts

      Paste tradelines straight from a credit report (closed and zero-balance accounts are left out; a mortgage line fills the current loan), or paste a free-text list. APR is optional, but adding it makes the blended rate and interest numbers exact.

    5. 5

      Run the savings analysis

      Pick the program (Standard, FHA Streamline, VA IRRRL), term, costs, skipped payments, and whether to roll debts in. Use Second lien (HELOAN) to leave the first mortgage alone, or Free & clear for a home with no mortgage.

    6. 6

      Compare (optional)

      Build side-by-sides of refi vs. home-equity options and hit Recommend on your pick.

    7. 7

      Send the proposal

      Check the programs to include and write one line on why you recommend it — it prints in a highlighted box. Copy proposal puts it on your clipboard as an image to paste into a text or email; Email, Save image and PDF are there too. Every version you send is kept in the client's proposal library with how it was sent.

    House rule: a line only appears on a client-facing proposal if it's a true benefit for that client's numbers. If the new loan costs more interest than staying put, "interest saved" simply doesn't show.

    Under the hood

    How the math works

    Every number on the analysis screen and the proposal comes from the same handful of formulas — nothing is hand-entered. In the formulas, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months.
    Monthly payment (P&I)used everywhere

    The standard fixed-rate mortgage payment.

    payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)

    P is the loan amount. At a 0% rate it's simply P ÷ n. Taxes and insurance aren't in this number — escrow is handled separately where it matters (see skipped payments).

    Amortization schedulepayoff time & total interest

    The toolkit walks the loan month by month:

    interest this month = balance × r principal this month = payment + extra − interest new balance = balance − principal

    It repeats until the balance hits zero, totaling the interest along the way. Any extra goes straight to principal. If a payment doesn't cover the month's interest, the result is "never pays off." This schedule feeds every "paid off in" and "total interest" figure and the balance chart.

    Months remaining from the original loanwhen there's no term left on file

    From the original amount, current balance and rate, the toolkit works out how far into the schedule the client is. It assumes a 30-year original term unless you pick another.

    P0 = payment on the original amount over N months k = ln((P0 − balance × r) ÷ (P0 − original × r)) ÷ ln(1 + r) months left = N − k
    Debt payoff time & interestcards, autos, personal loans

    How long each debt takes at what the client pays today (minimum plus any extra they already send), and what that costs:

    months = −ln(1 − balance × r ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + r) interest = payment × months − balance

    If the payment doesn't cover the monthly interest, it never pays off. If a debt has no APR, months are counted as balance ÷ payment (interest-free, so a floor) and the result is marked as an estimate.

    New loan amountwhat's being financed
    new loan = current balance + closing costs + upfront MIP + cash out + debts rolled in
    • Second lien (HELOAN): the first mortgage is untouched, so it's debts paid off + cash out + costs.
    • FHA Streamline: costs are lender-paid ($0); upfront MIP (1.75% of balance by default, editable) is financed; no debt roll-in or cash out.
    • VA IRRRL: rate-and-term only — no debt roll-in or cash out.
    Monthly savings"money out the door"
    out the door today = current P&I + debt minimums + extras they already pay (debts being rolled in) monthly savings = out the door today − new P&I

    For a second lien the first mortgage payment is the same on both sides, so it's left out of the comparison.

    Blended ratewhat their money really costs today

    A balance-weighted average of the mortgage rate and the APRs on the debts being consolidated:

    blended = (mortgage bal × rate + Σ debt bal × APR) ÷ (mortgage bal + Σ debt bal)

    Only debts with a known APR are included. On a HELOAN it's the debts only. This is what the new single rate is compared against ("one 6.25% loan replaces an 8.32% blended rate").

    Pay what you pay nowthe double-amortization play

    If the client keeps sending the same total they send today, the difference goes to principal every month:

    extra to principal = monthly savings schedule = amortize(new loan, new rate, term, extra)

    That gives the payoff time ("mortgage-free in 16 yrs 2 mo") and the interest actually paid. You can also set a custom extra (say $500) and the toolkit shows that payoff with the rest pocketed.

    Effective rate"6.25% on paper, 3.36% in practice"

    The rate a normal loan of the same amount and term would need to cost the same total interest the client actually pays with the extra. Found by searching (bisection) between 0% and the note rate:

    find x such that interest(amortize(new loan, x, term)) = interest actually paid with extra

    It illustrates total interest cost — it isn't a quoted rate or an APR.

    Interest savedonly shown when it's real
    today's path = interest left on the current mortgage + interest the debts cost at today's payments interest saved = today's path − interest on the new loan

    Shown on the proposal only if positive.

    Skipped paymentscash that stays in the account
    skipped cash = number of payments skipped × full PITI

    A skipped payment is the whole payment including escrow, not just P&I — the toolkit uses the total payment on file, or P&I + escrow. Not offered on FHA Streamlines, free & clear loans or seconds.

    APRon every proposal

    The rate that pays off the amount financed net of prepaid finance charges (closing costs + upfront MIP) at the actual monthly payment:

    find APR such that payment(new loan − finance charges, APR, n) = new payment

    With no costs entered it can't be computed, so the proposal shows note rate + 0.25% and marks it as an estimate.

    LTV, cash available & Max cashequity math
    LTV = balance ÷ home value cash available = home value × max LTV − what's owed Max cash (80%) = home value × 0.80 − costs − debts rolled in (rounded down to $1,000)

    LTV at or under 80% is noted as conventional cash-out territory — guidance, not a limit.

    Break-even, rate ladder & card payoffthe Tools deck
    break-even months = closing costs ÷ monthly savings rate ladder = P&I at the center rate and every ⅛% for four steps up and down card payoff = the debt payoff formula above, at the payment they make

    Good to know

    Your data & FAQ

    Where your work lives, what the office can see, and the questions that come up most.

    Where it lives

    Your save file on your computer is the source of truth. It's mirrored to the home-office hub as a backup whenever the hub is reachable, so a dead laptop doesn't lose your pipeline.

    Never collected

    SSN, date of birth, age and marital status are never captured, stored or sent anywhere — the Salesforce parser refuses those fields.

    What the office sees

    Your month's numbers and a once-a-minute "online" heartbeat (name and time only). Mirrored save files need IT's admin token to open.

    Why doesn't my payment match the servicer's statement?

    The toolkit's P&I excludes escrow. Enter the client's actual P&I if you have it — a typed P&I is never overwritten — and enter the total payment so skipped-payment cash uses the full PITI.

    Why is a debt's payoff marked "estimate"?

    That debt has no APR, so it's treated as interest-free. Add the APR from the credit report for an exact figure.

    Why can't I skip a payment or roll in debt on an FHA Streamline?

    Program rules: FHA Streamlines and VA IRRRLs are rate-and-term only, and FHA Streamlines don't skip a payment. The toolkit locks those fields so a proposal can't promise something the program won't do.

    Can I quote the effective rate?

    No. It illustrates total interest cost if the client keeps paying what they pay now. The note rate and APR are the quotable numbers.

    How do I get the newest version?

    The app updates itself — look for the Update bar. The Toolkit versions sidebar lists every recent change.
