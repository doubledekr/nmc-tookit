Getting started

The toolkit is a desktop app (Windows and Mac). Every calculation, chart, parser and PDF export runs on your own computer — no internet needed for the core work.

  1. Install the app

    IT or Dave installs it for you. It updates itself: when a new version is out you'll see an Update bar — click it, or it installs the next time you quit.

  2. Fill in the welcome card

    Your name, NMLS #, direct line and email. These print on every proposal and tag your call stats. The NMC main line (844-210-3644) is built in.

  3. Set your save file and the home office

    Settings → Your save file & the home office. Pick your save folder and paste the hub address IT gives you. A green dot means the hub is reachable. You do this once — after that saving, rate updates, team numbers and templates are automatic.

  4. Moving from the browser version?

    Use Settings → Import to bring in an old JSON export. Nothing is lost.

Off the VPN? Pipeline, client records, analysis, proposals and call-block logging all keep working off your local copy. Only shared things (rate pushes, team stats, the template library) pause, and they catch up on their own within a couple of minutes of reconnecting — including uploading anything you did offline.

Working a client, start to finish

Paste → parse → analyze → propose. About two minutes once you've done it a few times.

  1. Paste the Salesforce record

    Copy the client's NMC Salesforce profile, paste it in, hit Parse. You get a current → new preview of every field; check what you want and Apply. Debts in the record merge into the debt list by name. SSN, DOB, age and marital status are refused and never stored.

  2. Confirm the current loan

    Balance, rate and months remaining drive everything. If you only have the original amount, the toolkit back-solves months remaining (see the math below). Add home value for LTV.

  3. Paste lender pricing

    Paste a lender's rate stack; the parser reads rate, price or points, and lock period. Save the options you'd offer, then click one to use its rate in the analysis.

  4. Add debts

    Paste a credit report or a free-text list. For each line the largest number is read as the balance, the next as the payment, and a percent (or a decimal under 60) as the APR. APR is optional, but adding it makes the blended rate and interest numbers exact.

  5. Run the savings analysis

    Pick the program (Standard, FHA Streamline, VA IRRRL), term, costs, skipped payments, and whether to roll debts in. Use Second lien (HELOAN) to leave the first mortgage alone, or Free & clear for a home with no mortgage.

  6. Compare (optional)

    Build side-by-sides of refi vs. home-equity options, and hit Recommend on your pick.

  7. Send the proposal

    Check the programs to include, write one line on why you recommend it (it prints in a highlighted box), then Copy proposal — it lands on your clipboard as an image to paste into a text or email. Email, Save image and PDF are there too. Every version you send is kept in that client's proposal library with how it was sent.

House rule: a line only appears on a client-facing proposal if it's a true benefit for that client's numbers. If the new loan costs more interest than staying put, "interest saved" simply doesn't show.

How the toolkit calculates

Every number on the analysis screen and the proposal comes from the same handful of formulas — nothing is hand-entered. Open any one to see exactly how it works. In the formulas, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months.

Monthly payment (principal & interest) used everywhere

The standard fixed-rate mortgage payment.

payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)

P is the loan amount. At a 0% rate it's simply P ÷ n. Taxes and insurance are not in this number — escrow is handled separately where it matters (see skipped payments).

Amortization schedule payoff time & total interest

The toolkit walks the loan month by month:

interest this month = balance × r principal this month = payment + extra − interest new balance = balance − principal

It repeats until the balance hits zero, totaling the interest along the way. Any extra goes straight to principal. If a payment doesn't cover the month's interest, the result is "never pays off." This schedule is the source of every "paid off in" and "total interest" figure and the balance chart.

Months remaining from the original loan when Salesforce has no term left

From the original amount, current balance and rate, the toolkit works out how far into the schedule the client is. It assumes a 30-year original term unless you set another.

P0 = payment on the original amount over N months k = ln((P0 − balance × r) ÷ (P0 − original × r)) ÷ ln(1 + r) months left = N − k
Debt payoff time & interest cards, autos, personal loans

How long each debt takes at what the client pays today (minimum plus any extra they already send), and what that costs in interest:

months = −ln(1 − balance × r ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + r) interest = payment × months − balance

If the payment doesn't cover the monthly interest, it never pays off. If a debt has no APR, months are counted as balance ÷ payment (interest-free, so a floor) and the result is labeled an estimate.

New loan amount what's being financed
new loan = current balance + closing costs + upfront MIP + cash out + debts rolled in
  • Second lien (HELOAN): starts from zero — the first mortgage is untouched — so it's just debts paid off + cash out + costs.
  • FHA Streamline: costs are lender-paid ($0); upfront MIP (1.75% of balance by default, editable) is financed; no debt roll-in or cash out.
  • VA IRRRL: rate-and-term only; no debt roll-in or cash out.
Monthly savings "money out the door"
out the door today = current P&I + debt minimums + extras they already pay (debts being rolled in) monthly savings = out the door today − new P&I

For a second lien, the first mortgage payment stays the same on both sides, so it's left out of the comparison.

Blended rate what their money really costs today

A balance-weighted average of the mortgage rate and the APRs on the debts being consolidated:

blended = (mortgage bal × rate + Σ debt bal × APR) ÷ (mortgage bal + Σ debt bal)

Only debts with a known APR are included. This is the number the new single rate is compared against ("one 6.25% loan replaces an 8.32% blended rate").

Pay what you pay now the double-amortization play

If the client keeps sending the same total they send today, the difference between that and the new payment goes to principal every month:

extra to principal = monthly savings schedule = amortize(new loan, new rate, term, extra)

That gives the payoff time ("mortgage-free in 16 yrs 2 mo") and the interest actually paid. You can also set a custom extra (say $500) and the toolkit shows the payoff with the rest pocketed.

Effective rate "6.25% on paper, 3.36% in practice"

The rate a normal loan of the same amount and term would need to cost the same total interest the client actually pays with the extra. Found by searching (bisection) between 0% and the note rate:

find rate x such that interest(amortize(new loan, x, term)) = interest actually paid with extra

It's an illustration of total interest cost, not a quoted rate or an APR.

Interest saved only shown when it's real
today's path = interest left on the current mortgage + interest the debts cost at today's payments interest saved = today's path − interest on the new loan

Shown on the proposal only if positive.

Skipped payments cash that stays in the account
skipped cash = number of payments skipped × full PITI

A skipped payment is the whole payment including escrow, not just P&I — the toolkit uses the total payment from the record, or P&I + escrow. Not offered on FHA Streamlines, free & clear loans, or seconds. The Tools card caps it at 2 months.

APR Reg Z-style estimate

The rate that pays off the amount financed net of prepaid finance charges (closing costs + upfront MIP) at the actual monthly payment:

find APR such that payment(new loan − finance charges, APR, n) = new payment

With no costs entered, it can't be computed, so the proposal shows note rate + 0.25% and labels it an estimate.

LTV, cash available, and Max cash equity math
LTV = balance ÷ home value cash available = home value × max LTV − what's owed Max cash (80%) = home value × 0.80 − costs − debts rolled in (rounded down to $1,000)

LTV at or under 80% is flagged as conventional cash-out territory; over 97% as above standard limits.

Break-even, rate ladder, card payoff Tools deck
break-even months = closing costs ÷ monthly savings rate ladder = P&I at the center rate and every ⅛% for four steps up and down card payoff = debt payoff formula above, at the payment they make

Your data

Where it lives

Your save file on your computer is the source of truth. It's mirrored to the home-office hub as a backup whenever the hub is reachable, so a dead laptop doesn't lose your pipeline — IT can restore your last mirror.

What's never collected

SSN, date of birth, age and marital status are never captured, stored or sent anywhere — the Salesforce parser refuses those fields.

What the office sees

Call-block counts, your month's numbers, and a once-a-minute "online" heartbeat (name and timestamp only). Mirrored save files require IT's admin token to open.

FAQ

Why doesn't my payment match the servicer's statement?

The toolkit's P&I excludes escrow. Enter the client's actual P&I if you have it — it overrides the calculated one — and enter the total payment so skipped-payment cash uses the full PITI.

Why is a debt's payoff marked "estimate"?

That debt has no APR, so it's treated as interest-free. Add the APR from the credit report for an exact figure.

Why can't I skip a payment or roll in debt on an FHA Streamline?

Program rules: FHA Streamlines and VA IRRRLs are rate-and-term only, and FHA Streamlines don't skip a payment. The toolkit locks those fields so a proposal can't promise something the program won't do.

Is the effective rate something I can quote?

No. It illustrates total interest cost if the client keeps paying what they pay now. The note rate and APR are the quotable numbers.

How do I get the newest version?

The app updates itself — look for the Update bar. Release notes are on the releases page.
